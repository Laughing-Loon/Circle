Problem

Investing is a
social activity
built on entirely
individual rails.

Capital is inherently relational. Founders and investors build trust before any exchange. Communities form around shared values and a common understanding of the future they want to build.

Yet the infrastructure of investing — the accounts, the platforms, the legal vehicles — treats every investor as a solitary actor. Isolated. Unaccountable. Alone with their capital.

The next generation of investors feels this most acutely. Younger, less individually wealthy, and deeply values-driven, they are entering private markets at the exact moment those markets are democratising — but the coordination tools to act together don't exist.

Circle is the infrastructure they're missing: a platform that makes collective participation in private markets possible, purposeful, and governed.

01
Securities law isolates retail investors from private markets
Accredited investor requirements and SPV complexity create structural barriers that exclude most next-gen investors from the asset classes where wealth is actually built. Democratisation is happening — but without the community layer to make it meaningful.
02
True collective governance collapses under coordination costs
Shared decision-making is slow, expensive, and interpersonally fragile without the right infrastructure. Investment clubs dissolve. DAOs failed their promise. Multi-donor funds require institutional overhead most groups can't sustain. The governance layer is the missing piece.
03
Capital alienation: investors are disconnected from their own money
When investing is purely transactional and individual, capital loses its relational character. The next generation senses this and wants more — not just returns, but stewardship, accountability, and a genuine connection between their values and where their money goes.

Four structural shifts converging now

Shift 01
Capital as values
Increasingly, where capital goes is seen as a direct expression of identity. Next-gen investors don't just want returns — they want stewardship. Capital is action, not abstraction.
Shift 02
Collectivised decision-making
Growth in peer investment communities, syndicate ecosystems, and donor collaboratives signals genuine appetite for shared decision-making — not just individual tools with social overlays.
Shift 03
Private market democratisation
SPVs, rolling funds, ELTIF 2.0, and Reg CF are making alternatives accessible to smaller investors. The infrastructure exists. The community and governance layer to use it collectively does not.
Shift 04
AI elevates trust, not just access
AI is commoditising raw information advantage. What appreciates in value is human judgment, trusted curation, and authentic community. Private markets — where trust is the primary currency — are where this premium is highest.

Model 01
Social Layer
Community features wrap individual investing. Feeds, discussion, copy mechanics. Reduces isolation but doesn't change governance, ownership, or accountability.
→ eToro, Public.com, StockTwits
Model 02
Community of Practice
Peer networks for learning, norms, and curated access. Epistemic value is high. Portfolios remain individual — governance doesn't change.
→ Investment clubs, Syndicate networks
Model 03
Syndicate / SPV Pooling
Investors pool into a single vehicle for cap-table simplicity. Governance centralises with a lead GP. Collective participation — but not collective sovereignty.
→ AngelList, Republic, Carta
Model 04 — Circle's Territory
Pooled Capital with Shared Governance
Pooled capital and pooled decision-making with explicit governance processes. Multi-donor DAFs, cooperative vehicles, donor collaboratives. The highest form of collective investing — currently requiring institutional overhead.
→ Multi-donor DAFs, Collective Climate Justice Fund
Circle makes this accessible
Model 05
Place-Based Accountability
Community investment through CDFIs and investable notes. Investors accept mediated relationship with clearer geographic accountability. Institutional, slow, underfunded in tech.
→ CDFIs, Community foundations
Model 06
Ownership Redesign
Democratic ownership structures — CLTs, employee trusts, steward-ownership. Highest structural change; constrained liquidity. Long-horizon, not a platform play.
→ Community land trusts, Purpose trusts

Republic and AngelList have solved the infrastructure problem for individual access to private markets. DAOs attempted collective governance and failed — not because the idea was wrong, but because the product was wrong: crypto-native, ungoverned, and disconnected from real investment discipline.

No platform combines genuine collective governance, values intelligence, and a full private markets deployment stack. That intersection is Circle's founding territory. The question is not whether the demand exists — the wealth transfer data is unambiguous. The question is whether the governance layer can be made lightweight enough to scale.

Platform

Three deployment
tiers. One
governed stack.

01
Tier 1 · Launch
Collective Giving via DAF Sponsor
No securities registration required

The cleanest regulatory entry point. Circle facilitates collective grant and philanthropic investment decisions through users' existing DAF sponsors. Circle passes instructions — it never holds or moves capital. The governance and deliberation layer is entirely ours; the execution layer is the sponsor's.

  • Values profile matching surfaces relevant opportunities
  • Collective workspace: async deliberation, pledge signalling, lightweight vote
  • Grant instruction generation in DAF sponsor-compatible format
  • Capital types: grants, recoverable grants, programme-related investments (PRIs)
  • Phase 1b: direct API integration with ImpactAssets, Amalgamated Foundation
02
Tier 2 · 6–12 Months
Reg CF Partnership — Retail Access
Partner with registered funding portal

Circle partners with an existing Reg CF portal — Republic's impact vertical or a white-label arrangement — to open non-accredited investor participation. Circle owns the values intelligence and collective deliberation; the portal handles securities compliance. Unlocks the full next-gen cohort, not just accredited members.

  • $5M per offering ceiling per 12-month period
  • Opens participation to non-accredited investors
  • Capital types: debt notes, revenue-based finance, community equity
  • Enables place-based collective investment rounds — watersheds, local food systems, housing
03
Tier 3 · 18+ Months
Reg D SPV — Collective Investment Vehicle
Reg D 506(b) · Accredited investors only

A Reg D SPV pools accredited investors from a Circle collective into a single legal entity for one investment decision. Members contribute capital; the SPV makes the investment; members share governance and economic participation. Infrastructure via AngelList or Carta; Circle provides the values layer, collective governance, and diligence process on top.

  • Accredited investor verification per member
  • $5–15K legal cost per SPV — amortised across the collective
  • 5–10% carry on returns above hurdle (AngelList syndicate model)
  • Capital types: early-stage equity, impact debt, nature finance, co-investment
  • Pre-requisite: securities attorney confirmation and Reg D filing protocol
Product

Three pillars.
One platform
that turns values
into action.

01
Values & Identity Intelligence
A values profile engine that translates who you are and what you care about into a live investment lens — and matches you to people who share it.
  • 10-minute values onboarding: issue priorities, place-alignment, capital type preference, collective intent
  • ML-refined match scores across opportunities and co-investors
  • Narrative translation: turns financial data into stories investors connect with
  • AI-powered education layer: contextual explainers surfaced inside diligence cards
02
Collective Workspace & Governance
The infrastructure that makes shared decision-making lightweight enough to actually work — without collapsing under coordination costs or losing accountability.
  • Create or join a named collective (3–20 members)
  • Shared opportunity feed filtered by collective values alignment
  • Async deliberation: discussion threads, interest signals, structured debate
  • Lightweight vote with commitment thresholds and revocable pledges
  • Accountability layer: post-deployment impact tracking, member reporting
03
Curated Opportunity Discovery
A sourced, diligenced pipeline of private market opportunities — ranked by values-match score, not just financial returns. Expert-curated, not user-generated.
  • Expert-curated diligence cards: financials, impact thesis, team, use of funds
  • Values-alignment rationale written for each opportunity
  • Filterable by capital type, deal size, geography, theme, and collective history
  • Community signal layer: anonymised aggregate interest, no individual exposure
How it works → a user journey
Step 01
Build your values profile
10-minute onboarding captures what you care about, how you want to act, and who you want to act with. This is your investment identity — refined over time.
Step 02
Join or form a Circle
Find peers with aligned values and investment intent. Your Circle is a named, governed group — not a feed. 3–20 members. Real relationships, real decisions.
Step 03
Deliberate on curated opportunities
Your shared feed surfaces opportunities matched to your Circle's collective values profile. Deliberate asynchronously. Signal interest. Read expert diligence. Build consensus.
Step 04
Deploy together
From grant instruction to Reg D SPV — execute the collective decision through the appropriate deployment tier. Track impact. Close the loop. Do it again.
